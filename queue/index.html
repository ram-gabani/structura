What is a Queue?

While a stack restricts operations to one single open end (LIFO), a Queue is a two-ended linear data structure where elements are inserted at one end and removed from the opposite end.

New elements join the line at the REAR (tail), while existing elements are served and removed from the FRONT (head). This guarantees fair ordering: FIFO (First In, First Out).

FIFO Principle
First In, First Out
The earliest element inserted at the rear is always the first one eligible for deletion at the front.
FRONT Pointer
Service & Deletion
Tracks the head element. Dequeue operations advance the FRONT pointer forward.
REAR Pointer
Arrival & Insertion
Tracks the tail element. Enqueue operations advance the REAR pointer forward.
⚡ O(1) Speed
Direct Indexing
Both enqueue and dequeue operate in constant time without shifting items.

💡 The Pointer Movement Rules
  • Enqueue: Increments rear forward (rear++) and stores the new value at arr[rear].
  • Dequeue: Extracts the value at arr[front] and increments front forward (front++).
  • Empty Queue Condition: When initially created, front = -1 and rear = -1 (or front > rear after all items are removed).

Real-Life Analogies: Fair FIFO Service

Real-world systems where cutting the line is strictly forbidden

🎟️ Movie Theater
Cinema Ticket Counter
The first moviegoer to arrive stands at the front of the window and gets served first. Late arrivals join the tail of the line. Nobody in the middle gets tickets ahead of those in front.
🖨️ OS Print Spooler
Network Printer Queue
When 10 students send PDF lab reports to the university shared printer simultaneously, the print spooler executes documents in the exact order they arrived over the network.
🚗 Single-Lane Highway
Toll Booth Lane
Cars enter a narrow one-lane toll plaza. The car that reached the toll gate first pays and departs first. Overtaking inside the lane is physically impossible.
